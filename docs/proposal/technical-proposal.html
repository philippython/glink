1 · Executive summary

A working platform, ready to be hardened and scaled.

Glink is a two-sided UGC marketplace: brands write one brief, Glink matches it to the creators most likely to deliver, checks every video against the brief before a brand sees it, and pays creators from escrow on approval — with automatic bonuses when an ad wins. The core product is built and tested. This proposal sets out how we take it to production and then to scale, with particular attention to payments and video storage.

2 · Architecture: today and target

Stateless servers, managed data, video off the request path.

The target keeps the same code and API, but moves state out of the server process. Large files never pass through the app servers, slow work runs in background workers, and any app server can answer any request — so we can add servers as traffic grows.

ChangeChoiceReason
DatabaseManaged Postgres (e.g. Neon, Render or Supabase) with point-in-time recoveryMultiple servers, concurrent writes, backups to the minute. The SQL is close to SQLite’s, so the move is a contained migration
Object storageCloudflare R2 (S3-compatible)No egress fees — the dominant cost of video at scale; S3 API keeps us portable to AWS S3
CDNCloudflare in front of the app and R2Fast global delivery of the app and video; signed URLs keep videos private
Events & jobsRedis: pub/sub for live updates, BullMQ queues, shared rate limitsLive updates reach users on any server; retries and back-off for every background job
WorkersSame codebase, separate process: transcode, pre-flight, email, webhooks, reconciliation, the payout sweepKeeps API latency low and isolates heavy CPU work
HostingRender (or Fly/Railway) web + worker services; optional AWS laterLow operational load now; containers move anywhere
Today: one Node service serves the app and API, with SQLite and videos on a persistent disk — ideal for a pilot, but limited to one server, uploads buffered in memory, and data that only survives if a disk is attached (on Render’s free plan it is wiped on every restart). The target removes each limit. Serverless hosts (Netlify, Vercel) suit only isolated pieces, not the core.

Do we need Supabase?

Not for authentication — Glink’s own auth already works and is tested; the sign-in problems seen on Render came from the free plan wiping its data on restart. Supabase is a good option as a managed Postgres (with a generous free tier) when we move off SQLite. Its Storage is an alternative to R2, but R2’s free egress is cheaper for video.

Design principles

Standard protocols (S3, SQL, OAuth, webhooks) over proprietary APIs; managed services for anything stateful; every vendor behind a small adapter (payments, email, storage, AI) so it can be swapped without touching product code.

5 · The matching engine

A score anyone can read, learned from results.

Every creator gets a 0–99 score for every campaign: a weighted sum of eight factors, each producing a plain-language reason. It is a set of pure, unit-tested functions, so weights can be tuned safely as real data arrives.

Who gets invited

  • Unavailable creators, or rates above twice the fee, are not eligible.
  • Auto-match invites eligible creators scoring 55+, up to the number wanted; a decline invites the next best.
  • Availability multiplies the score: Limited × 0.85; three or more active jobs × 0.8.

6 · Pre-flight checks and AI

Catch problems before a brand spends time on them.

Each upload is checked against its brief in layers. Anything that can’t be verified is marked “check by eye” — the system never guesses. In the target architecture these checks run in a background worker on the verified file.

LayerChecksStatus
1 · FileLength within range; aspect ratio within 3% of the brief; resolution (1080p passes, 720p review, lower fails); MP4/MOVBuilt moves server-side with ffprobe (Phase 2)
2 · ScriptMust-include lines and avoid words (quoted words plus a built-in medical-claims list), with estimated timestampsBuilt
3 · VisionUp to 8 sampled frames judged by Claude for visual items (“show the texture”), returning pass, fail or unclear with a timestampBuilt optional, needs an API key
4 · SpeechSpeech-to-text transcript with word timings, so spoken lines are checked without a creator-supplied script; music-over-voice detectionPhase 4 provider such as Deepgram or AssemblyAI

Scoring

Brief match (0–100) = 20 × format score + 80 × brief score, where each item counts pass 1, review 0.6, fail 0.

AI cost control

Frames are down-sampled and capped at eight; one structured-output call per upload; AI can be turned off per environment. Planned: cache results per file so re-checks of the same upload are free. Per-review token cost is measured during the pilot.

Failure handling

If an AI or transcription call fails, the deterministic results stand and the item is marked for a human — uploads are never blocked by a third party.

7 · Video storage and delivery

Upload straight to storage. Verify on the server. Stream privately.

Video is the heaviest thing Glink handles and the asset brands pay for. The plan takes large files off the app servers entirely, verifies what was uploaded, produces fast previews, and keeps originals safe for download.

How it works

  • Resumable direct upload. The API issues short-lived, presigned multipart URLs; the browser uploads 10 MB parts straight to R2 and retries failed parts. Files up to 2 GB; no server memory used.
  • Server-side verification. A worker runs ffprobe on the stored file — real duration, resolution, codec and audio — replacing browser-reported values.
  • Renditions. ffmpeg makes a 720p review copy (fast start), a poster image and the frames for the AI check. The original is never altered.
  • Private playback. Signed Cloudflare links that expire in minutes; brands download the original on approval.
  • Safety. File type checked by content, not name; optional moderation scan.

Why R2? Storage at these volumes costs tens of dollars a month; what gets expensive with video is egress (every playback), which R2 doesn’t charge. Its S3 API keeps AWS S3 + CloudFront open, and a managed service such as Mux remains a per-minute shortcut if we need adaptive streaming later.
8 · Payments: money in, money out

Stripe moves the money. The ledger explains it.

Brands top up a wallet by card; campaign budgets are held when a campaign launches; fees release on approval and become withdrawable after three days; bonuses and prizes follow the same path. Stripe handles card data, identity checks and bank payouts, so Glink never touches card or bank details.

Model. “Separate charges and transfers”: the platform collects the payment, records it in the ledger, and transfers to creators when they withdraw. 9 · Automated escrow

Money moves on its own — and only when it should.

Nobody chases a payment: a background job runs every minute and moves money forward, while the ledger and idempotency keys make sure nothing is paid twice. Built and covered by automated tests.

How others do it

Upwork releases a fixed-price milestone automatically if the client doesn’t respond within 14 days. Fiverr auto-completes an order 3 days after delivery and clears seller funds after a holding period. Glink uses 5 days to approve and 3 days to clear.

Safe by construction

Webhook events stored once; a checkout credits once; each transfer has an idempotency key; one payout in flight per creator; failed payouts retry on the next run and never lose earnings. At scale the same jobs move to a Redis queue (BullMQ).

10 · Payment controls and compliance

Correct under retries, auditable, and ready for more markets.

Built today

  • Webhooks verified by signature; each event stored once (stripe_events).
  • A checkout session can credit a wallet only once (unique external_id).
  • Transfers use idempotency keys; one withdrawal in flight per creator.
  • Payout account readiness tracked from account.updated.
  • Friendly handling when Stripe’s available balance is short — earnings stay safe.
  • Automated payment and escrow tests run on every change.

Proposed (Phase 1 and 5)

  • Platform fee configured per plan and shown on every payout statement.
  • Refunds of unused wallet balance to the original card, recorded in the ledger.
  • Disputes: charge.dispute.created freezes the disputed amount and alerts the owner.
  • Nightly reconciliation of ledger totals against Stripe balance transactions, with alerts on any difference.
  • Statements and receipts (PDF) for brands; earnings statements for creators.
  • Audit log of every money action and who performed it.

Compliance

TopicApproach
Card data (PCI DSS)Card entry happens on Stripe-hosted Checkout, so Glink’s scope is the lightest (SAQ A). No card numbers touch Glink servers.
Identity (KYC)Stripe Connect verifies creators and screens payouts; Glink stores only the connected-account ID and readiness.
Holding fundsFunds sit in the platform’s Stripe balance, tracked per brand and campaign. Wording such as “escrow” and holding periods to be confirmed with legal counsel per launch country.
TaxStripe’s tax forms for creator earnings where applicable (e.g. 1099 in the US); VAT/sales tax on platform fees via Stripe Tax if required.

More markets: a provider adapter

Stripe is not available to platforms based in Nigeria and much of Africa, and payouts to some countries are restricted. We will put Stripe behind a small PaymentProvider interface — create top-up · verify webhook · onboard payee · transfer · refund — and add Paystack (cards, bank transfer and local payouts in Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya and South Africa) as a second implementation. Each brand and creator is routed by country and currency; the ledger records the currency of every entry.

11 · Security and privacy

Secure by default, with less to protect.

The best protection is not holding sensitive data: card and bank details stay with Stripe, and videos are private by default. What Glink does hold is protected at every layer.

Privacy and data protection Phase 1 & 5

Designed for GDPR (EU/UK) and Nigeria’s NDPA: a privacy policy and terms at launch; self-serve data export and account deletion; data minimisation; processors (Stripe, Resend, Cloudflare, AI and speech providers) listed with data-processing agreements. Creator videos will not be used to train third-party models.

Content rights Phase 1

Each campaign will record the licence a brand receives (platforms, duration, paid usage, territories), which creators accept before filming. Approval and payment stamp the licence start; expiry reminders help brands stay compliant; paid-partnership disclosure (#ad) becomes a default brief item.
